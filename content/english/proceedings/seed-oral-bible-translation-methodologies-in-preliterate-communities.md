For communities with strong oral traditions and limited literacy, conventional written translation processes are insufficient. This paper describes a community-based oral translation methodology piloted in three communities in West Africa. The methodology centers storytelling, call-and-response checking, and audio recording rather than written drafts. Evaluation data from 18 months of implementation shows significantly higher community comprehension compared to a parallel written translation project in an adjacent community.
