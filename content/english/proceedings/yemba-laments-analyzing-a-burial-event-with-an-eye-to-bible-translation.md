<p>This thesis examines the content and role of Yemba lament songs (Cameroon). A<br> 4 day burial event was recorded, and Schrag’s (2013) seven lens descriptive approach is<br> used, to provide a detailed description of the event. Conceptual Metaphor Theory is used<br> to examine the database of 109 song extracts, leading to the identification of a primary<br> metaphor, LIFE IS A JOURNEY. Though metaphor is the most frequent poetic device<br> (32%), other poetic devices occur and are discussed: metonymy, rhetorical questions,<br> irony, etc. Finally, turning to issues of Bible translation, aspects of Zogbo and Wendland<br> (2020) are discussed. A biblical lament, (2 Sam 1:19-27), is analyzed and used to<br> compare Hebrew and Yemba inventories of poetic devices and themes. The results reveal<br> a great deal of overlap. It is suggested that such comparisons of lament texts would benefit MotherTongue Translators as they prepare for translating.</p>
