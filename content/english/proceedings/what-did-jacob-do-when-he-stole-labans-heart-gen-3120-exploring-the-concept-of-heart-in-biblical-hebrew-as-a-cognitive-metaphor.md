<p>For the Key Terms of the Old Testament project, I researched the word 'lev' (heart). I ended up in a web of seemingly unrelated idioms where heart always seems to mean something different.</p> <p>For example, in Gen 31:20 Jacob stole Laban's heart. All translations seem to agree that this means that Jacob deceived Laban. But in Gen 34:3, speaking to the heart means to speak tenderly, romantically. The heart is a place of secret thoughts (Ecc. 7:5), but it can also mean a place in the middle of the sea (Pro 30:19). How can one word point to trickery, tenderness, hiddenness, and a location, all at the same time?</p> <p>Analysing the concept of 'lev' as a conceptual metaphor shows a continuum between the senses that gives insight in how the writers of the Bible looked at the heart. Understanding what the word 'lev' really meant for them can enable us to find a more meaningful way of translating these idioms into our own languages.</p> <p>Bibliography:<br> Di Vito, R.
