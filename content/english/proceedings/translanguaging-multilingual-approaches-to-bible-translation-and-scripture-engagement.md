<p>Members of grassroots multilingual communities conceptualize language differently than primary monolinguals do. This creates challenges because most Bible agencies model monolingual approaches to engaging with the Bible, whether they emphasize gateway languages or vernacular languages. Grassroots multilinguals often prefer an unbounded, performative approach to languages that reflects their dynamic, hybridized identities; one term for this is translanguaging. Translanguagers make meaning by using words from their full linguistic repertoire. Using translinguistic theories and methodologies, Bible agencies can implement translation strategies that are more appropriate for some multilingual communities. It is also critical to successful Scripture engagement to model multilingual approaches that more naturally correspond to local language practices. Translanguaging offers methodological insights from multilingual education that are particularly useful for Scripture engagement application.</p>
